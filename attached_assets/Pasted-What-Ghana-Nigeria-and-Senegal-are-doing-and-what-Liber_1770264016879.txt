What Ghana, Nigeria, and Senegal are doing (and what Liberia should copy)
Ghana (GLMIS + inclusive AI job-matching)

From the GLMIS portal itself, Ghana’s system covers job seeker registration + jobs, counselling bookings, training providers, employer vacancy posting, accident reporting, employment agencies / public employment centres, plus a statistics area with labour-market indicators and dashboards.
Ghana also pioneered inclusive matching via USSD through Text4Jobs (AI-enabled matching, USSD access, call-center support, SMS notifications) to avoid excluding people without smartphones or internet.

Nigeria (NELEX + employment exchanges + labour-market intelligence)

Nigeria’s Ministry of Labour describes a network of Employment Exchanges that register job seekers, canvass vacancies, place applicants, and collect/analyze employment statistics, including career guidance and occupational classification work; and it explicitly links this to NELEX as the online matching and labour market information platform.
At subnational level, Lagos’ LMIS emphasizes being a one-stop platform for labour-market data and insights (sectors/occupations, in-demand skills, salary levels, training program guidance), drawing on online job postings and interviews.

Senegal (ANPEJ SIME + youth services + jobseeker ID)

ANPEJ’s “EmploiJeunes” portal exposes recent jobs, and services for job seekers including orientation, project support (entrepreneurship), migration interest, and training search, plus employer services (recruitment / posting).
Senegal’s ANPEJ launch coverage highlights SIME providing a map/cartography of job seekers, access for enterprises and partners to post offers, and issuance of a job seeker card.

My honest take on your “what you developed is far below” point

You’re right if we compare only against the “employment services / job portal” layer (vacancies, placement workflows, counselling bookings, training provider marketplace, USSD inclusion, jobseeker card). Ghana/Nigeria/Senegal clearly have those service features.

But Liberia can still become #1 in West Africa by building a platform that is both:

a National Labour Exchange & Services (what they have), and

a National Job Creation Observatory with verification, trust scoring, and real-time job creation measurement across public + private sectors (what they generally do not do at high rigor).

That “verified job creation ledger” is the differentiator that makes Liberia outstanding.

Liberia’s “#1 in West Africa” platform: the superset blueprint
Pillar A — Labour Exchange & Citizen Services (copy + improve)

Job seeker registry, profiles/CVs, vacancy posting, applications, interviews, placement outcomes (NELEX-style).

Counselling/career guidance appointment booking (GLMIS-style).

Training provider & course registry + enrollments + certifications (GLMIS/Lagos LMIS logic).

Public Employment Centres + Private Employment Agencies workflows and reporting (GLMIS + Nigeria’s exchanges).

Inclusive channels: USSD/SMS + call-center assisted registration (Text4Jobs model).

Youth entrepreneurship + migration services (ANPEJ-style service bundles).

Pillar B — National Job Creation Observatory (Liberia’s differentiator)

Track job creation as “employment spells” (start/end, employer, hours, wage band, county, occupation/industry).

Verification engine (multi-source): employer attestations + sampled audits + payroll/tax signals + project reports + survey calibration.

Job Creation Trust Score per record and per employer/project (e.g., Verified / Partially Verified / Self-Reported).

Public sector + donor-funded projects: required job reporting by contracts/grants (with geo-tagging by county).

Dashboards: jobs created (gross/net), retention (30/90/180 days), youth/women inclusion, wage distribution, formal/informal estimates.

Pillar C — Standards, analytics, and policymaking (international best practice)

Statistical standards and metadata aligned to ILO concepts and labour-stat guidance.

Use standard classifications:

Occupations: ISCO-08 concepts and coding.

Industries: ISIC Rev.4.

Privacy-by-design principles (data minimization, purpose limitation, etc.).
